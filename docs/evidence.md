# Evidence for Cafetal: the figures behind the problem

This file holds the numbers behind Cafetal's problem statement for **Kirinyaga County, central Kenya** (the Mt Kenya coffee belt). It covers who Noor is, what threatens her coffee, how far away advice is, what phone and network she has, what language support exists for Gĩkũyũ and Kiswahili, and what price references exist. Noor, the Ondera Farmers' Co-operative Society and its four communities are fictional. The figures are about the real Kenya around them.

Every row gives the value, unit, year, place, source and URL. Each row also says whether the figure is **measured** (census, survey, administrative record, observed market price) or **modelled/estimated** (forecast, model output, operator-declared coverage), and how far we were able to check it.

**How we checked (read this first).** The build machine's network blocks almost every statistics site. KNBS, the Communications Authority of Kenya (CA), GSMA, the World Bank, FAO/FAOSTAT, USDA FAS, KALRO, AFA, KAMIS, HDX, Wikipedia, the Kenyan press and OpenCelliD all failed through both the web-fetch tool and curl (proxy policy denial, tested 2026-10-04). We could read only GitHub files and the Hugging Face Hub (through its connector) directly. So most rows below rest on **web-search result summaries**. We never opened those pages, so a teammate with normal internet must click the URL before a number is quoted on stage or in the video. Where two sources disagree, we show both.

Verification codes used in the tables:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **F** | Fetched: read directly on the primary source or its official metadata during this session |
| **S** | Search snippet only: seen in a search-engine summary; the page itself was not opened. **Unverified: check before recording.** |
| **D** | Derived by us from cited figures (the arithmetic is shown). A D row is only as strong as its inputs: if an input is S, check it before recording |
| **L** | Local: the hackathon concept note (`docs/concept-note.pdf`) or a file in this repo |

## 10. Comparison with the two examples in the concept note

The concept note (Annex B) points to **Wadhwani AI's cotton pest tool** (CottonAce) and to **World Bank-supported digital advisory in Côte d'Ivoire**. With CottonAce, a farmer photographs the insects caught in a pheromone trap; the app counts pink bollworms and gives a threshold-based spray advisory. Reported results are up to 26.5% higher profit margins and 38% lower pesticide spending in an independent review, and up to 22% higher income in 2020–21 in Ranebennur (Karnataka) and Wardha (Maharashtra). There was **no significant benefit in a 2021–22 multistate trial**, because unusually heavy rain kept pest pressure low (search snippets only, code S: https://aiopportunity.wadhwaniai.org/main-page-dev-lib/cottonace , https://www.fastcompany.com/90640843/google-is-helping-deploy-ai-to-prevent-pests-devastating-indian-crops ). Côte d'Ivoire's **e-Agriculture Project** (P160418, US$70 M, 2018–2023) shows what gets advice to farmers in the first place: registries and connectivity. It reports more than 400,000 people with better market access through the Agristore platform, mobile broadband for more than 221,000 rural people, more than 43,000 new mobile money accounts, and more than 11 million advisory messages, about 44% of them to women (code S: https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2025/03/04/afw-from-fields-to-markets-the-role-of-digital-platforms-in-west-africa-agricultural-success ; PAD https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/900251527478271533/pdf/COTE-DIVOIRE-PADnew-05082018.pdf ). Cafetal copies the CottonAce pattern (one photo, on-device vision, one action this week) but classifies leaf symptoms instead of counting insects, and hands uncertain cases to a person. It takes the Côte d'Ivoire lesson at co-op scale: in Kirinyaga the co-operative society and its factories already know their members, so a consented member registry plus SMS is what turns a diagnosis into an extension visit. The year CottonAce showed no benefit is a reminder that advice must stay humble when conditions change.

**Lab-to-field gap (verified):** a model trained on 54,306 controlled-condition leaf images reached 99.35% on its held-out test set but **31.4%** on images taken in other conditions (Mohanty, Hughes & Salathé 2016, arXiv:1604.03169, abstract read via HF Papers: **F**). This is why Cafetal reports accuracy on field photos separately from its Kenyan test split, and why it answers "I'm not sure — show the leaf to the extension officer." when unsure.

## Gaps and caveats

1. **Almost every Kenya figure is snippet-level (S).** The build machine could not open KNBS, CA, GSMA, World Bank, FAO, USDA, AFA, KALRO, KAMIS or the Kenyan press. The verified rows (F) are the AI-language facts (§8), the JMuBEN metadata (§3b), Kenya's mobile country code (§7) and the Mohanty lab-to-field result (§10). Before the video, someone must open the URL behind every number we show on screen.
2. **Same county, different conditions.** JMuBEN was photographed in Kirinyaga, Noor's county, so the variety mix, pests and light are close to hers. But it comes from one plantation with a pathologist, and it is close-up crops. Our field-photo test (64.2% of held-out rust photos correct, `reports/field_eval.md`, L) uses iNaturalist photos that are **all from the Americas**: we computed from `reports/field_inat_attribution.csv` that 0 of the 162 field-test photos fall in Kenya. The only Kenyan photos in that file are 4 of the 399 *Coffea* plant photos used to count false alarms. **No accuracy figure for Kenyan field photos taken on a phone exists yet.**
3. **The model cannot see coffee berry disease.** It is Kenya's other major coffee disease (§4) and attacks berries, not leaves. It also cannot see berry borer, nutrient deficiency, drought or old trees, any of which could explain Noor's falling yield.
4. **No FAOSTAT pull.** Kenyan official figures are clean coffee by crop year; USDA figures are bag forecasts by marketing year, and USDA revises them (the MY2024/25 figure changed from 750,000 to about 950,000 bags between the 2025 and 2026 reports). KIPPRA's "70% decline" does not match its own endpoints (62%).
5. **Measured vs modelled.** The census, the 2023/24 Kenya Housing Survey, FinAccess, Findex, the literacy survey and the AFA/KNBS production counts are measured. GSMA regional gaps are survey-based models. CA's 97.3% 4G coverage is **operator-declared population coverage, not measured signal**. USDA crop numbers are estimates and forecasts. CA's device and mobile-money counts are per SIM or device, not per person.
6. **Language counts are proxies.** Kenya's census records ethnicity, not language: 8.1 million Kikuyu is not 8.1 million Gĩkũyũ speakers. No literacy figure exists *in* Gĩkũyũ. Our Kiswahili and Gĩkũyũ card texts are AI drafts and stay "unverified" until native speakers review them (`content/README.md`).
7. **Voice models exist but carry conditions.** MMS has Gĩkũyũ and Kiswahili TTS and ASR, but under a non-commercial licence, trained on Bible readings, at about 145 MB per TTS voice (too big for the phone, fine for pre-rendering at the hub). Its quality on farming words is unknown.
8. **Prices are not farm-gate in Noor's valley.** NCE prices are USD per 50 kg of clean coffee; payouts are KES per kg of cherry, per factory, months after delivery. Maize snippets conflict (KES 4,831 vs under KES 2,200 per bag in 2026). Market-price dates are often missing from the snippets.
9. **Gender data is thin for Kenya.** Recent GSMA Kenya-only numbers conflict between snippets. The strongest Kenyan gender figures are from the 2023/24 survey (rural women's internet use 21.7% vs 28.3% for rural men).
10. **Looked for and not found:** Findex rural account ownership for Kenya; extension officers per farmer in Kirinyaga; the share of farm households visited by extension; the national share of coffee area under Ruiru 11/Batian; literacy in Gĩkũyũ; a census count of Gĩkũyũ speakers; OpenCelliD tower counts around Ondera.

- **Prices:** for coffee, use the NCE weekly sale results (the Exchange's own results page or AFA Coffee Directorate) and the payout list your co-op society publishes. For maize and beans, use KAMIS and choose one named market (for example Kerugoya, Kirinyaga) and the same commodity variety every week.
- **Kenyan field photos:** the most useful evidence still missing. Ask a Kirinyaga co-op or KALRO-CRI for 50 to 100 consented phone photos of rust, leaf miner and healthy leaves, labelled by an extension officer, and score the shipped model on them without changing the threshold.

## Problem statement (template from PROJECT_BRIEF.md §3, filled for Kenya)

**Recommended statement:**

> Because of Cafetal, **Noor** will **get a sick-looking coffee leaf onto the extension officer's visit list, with advice she can follow in Gĩkũyũ, Kiswahili or English while she waits,** by **the same weekend she notices it**, which she would otherwise **do late: whenever the officer next reaches her area, twice a year at best**; we know because **that is the gap the challenge brief describes for her (concept note, Annex B, 2026), and Kenya has about one public extension worker for every 1,380 farmers, against its own target of 1:600 (Ministry of Agriculture, 2023–2025; check before recording).**

**Why it is worded this way:** the sentence promises a place on the officer's list, not a diagnosis. The shipped model (v2 at threshold 0.90, shipped by team decision as an exception to the ship rule) names rust in 34 of 53 held-out field photos (64.2%), leaf miner in 2 of 9 and Cercospora in 0 of 10. It also gives a disease answer for 2.5% of photos of coffee plants (all measured, `reports/field_eval.md`, L). Those field photos come from the Americas; on Kenyan photos the field accuracy is unmeasured (gap 2). A disease or "not sure" answer puts the farm on the officer's list (a "healthy" answer does not), and the officer decides, so the sentence holds. Note that the Kiswahili and Gĩkũyũ advice texts are AI drafts awaiting native-speaker review.

**Evidence for "we know because", strongest first:**

- **L:** "The nearest extension officer visits the sub-county twice a year at best" (concept note, Annex B, 2026).
- **F:** the model learned from JMuBEN, Kenyan Arabica leaves (AgML metadata). *Check before recording:* the photos were taken at Mutira, Kirinyaga, Noor's own county (Jepkoech et al. 2021, S).
